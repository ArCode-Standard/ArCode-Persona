# Moroccan Wardrobe Catalog — Research-First Plan

## Design principle

Moroccan identity is diverse and cannot be represented by a single costume, region, gender presentation, or skin tone. The avatar studio must offer heritage-inspired and contemporary options without inferring a person's identity from their appearance.

## Initial garment families

| Family | Intended customization dimensions | Research requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Djellaba | hood, cut, sleeve, length, fabric, trim | Regional and everyday variations |
| Caftan | silhouette, sfifa-inspired trim, belt, fabric, occasion | Construction and ceremonial context |
| Takchita | layered garment components, belt, embroidery | Layering and occasion-specific references |
| Contemporary Moroccan | modern streetwear, casual and formal looks | Current local style references |
| Accessories | babouches, fez, scarves, belts, jewelry, eyewear | Context and appropriate use |

## Visual language

Potential material and pattern references include woven textiles, sfifa-style edging, embroidery, buttons, metallic thread, and regionally sourced motifs. No motif should be presented as universally Moroccan without a documented source.

## Required review before production assets

1. Collect references from multiple Moroccan regions and communities.
2. Record the garment or accessory name, source, region (when known), context, and date.
3. Have references reviewed by knowledgeable cultural contributors.
4. Separate documented tradition from contemporary reinterpretation.
5. Avoid sacred, ceremonial, or community-specific motifs used as generic decoration.
6. Keep names and cultural descriptions factual, reviewed, and editable.

## Prototype status

The current studio uses simple geometric placeholder meshes and color accents. These are not accurate garment simulations or culturally validated representations. The catalog is a design and research backlog, not a claim that production assets exist.
