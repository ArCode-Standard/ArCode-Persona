# Moroccan Identity & Wardrobe Direction

## Product intent

Moroccan cultural references should be represented as a broad, respectful, configurable wardrobe and visual-language collection. The collection must not imply that one outfit, region, gender expression, or appearance represents every Moroccan person.

## Initial wardrobe families

- **Djellaba:** silhouette and hood variations, seasonal fabrics, embroidery and trim.
- **Caftan:** formal garment variations, sfifa-style trim references, belts and layered details.
- **Takchita:** layered formal styling, with careful separation of garment layers.
- **Modern Moroccan:** contemporary everyday clothing, with optional locally inspired patterns and accessories.
- **Accessories:** babouches, fez, scarves, belts, jewelry and optional eyewear.

## Visual research and review

Before production asset creation:
1. Gather references across Moroccan regions and communities.
2. Document garment names, construction details, materials and contexts.
3. Review references with knowledgeable cultural contributors.
4. Avoid treating ceremonial, regional or gendered garments as generic costumes.
5. Provide modern and non-traditional choices alongside heritage clothing.
6. Keep cultural descriptions factual and editable; do not infer a user's identity from appearance.

## Prototype limitations

The current browser prototype uses procedural geometric meshes and stylized placeholder shapes. It demonstrates interaction and broad wardrobe concepts only. It is not a production-grade character model, does not reproduce authentic tailoring or embroidery, and has no validated cultural asset library.

## Future asset requirements

Production assets should include consistent topology, UVs, PBR materials, LODs, rig compatibility, cloth deformation tests, and documented provenance/licensing. Garment and accessory assets should be modular and validated against supported body rigs.
